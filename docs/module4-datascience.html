Day 1, Session 1: The Data Science Process & Exploration

GNEST305 Module 4 — Basics of Data Science, Lifecycle, Big Data & Visual Exploration (2.0 Hours)

1 Why Start with Module 4?

Before we decompose matrices (Module 2), derive Maximum Likelihood Estimators (Module 3), or train Deep Neural Networks (Module 1), an AI engineer must understand how raw observations become a structured data matrix \(X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}\) and how the Data Science Process guides every modeling decision.

2 1. The 6-Step Data Science Process (Cielen & Meysman)

In Introducing Data Science (Text-5), the lifecycle of any data science or engineering AI project follows six iterative stages:

  1. Setting the Research Goal: Define the problem, context, deliverable, and success metrics (e.g., Can we classify flower species or detect motor bearing wear with \(>95\%\) accuracy?).
  2. Retrieving Data: Collect internal/external data from databases, CSV logs, or IoT sensors.
  3. Data Preparation (Cleansing, Transforming, Combining): Handle missing values (NaN), outliers, unit mismatches, and feature scaling (\(z\)-score standardization).
  4. Data Exploration (EDA): Compute statistical summaries, plot histograms, boxplots, scatter matrices, and correlation heatmaps to spot patterns and anomalies.
  5. Data Modeling (Statistics + Machine Learning): Select features, split into Training and Testing sets, fit statistical/ML models, and evaluate generalization error.
  6. Presentation & Automation: Communicate findings via dashboards (Tableau, PowerBI, Quarto) or deploy automated real-time inference pipelines.

3 2. Primary Thread: Exploring & Preparing the Iris Data Matrix

Every dataset in AI starts as a feature matrix \(X \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}\) and (for supervised tasks) a target vector \(y \in \mathbb{R}^n\):

\[X = \begin{bmatrix} x_{11} & x_{12} & \dots & x_{1d} \\ x_{21} & x_{22} & \dots & x_{2d} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ x_{n1} & x_{n2} & \dots & x_{nd} \end{bmatrix}, \quad y = \begin{bmatrix} y_1 \\ y_2 \\ \vdots \\ y_n \end{bmatrix}\]

For the Iris dataset, \(n = 150\) flowers and \(d = 4\) continuous morphological measurements (in cm). Notice how features have different scales and variances—which is why Step 3 (Data Preparation) requires Standardization (\(z\)-score scaling):

\[z_{ij} = \frac{x_{ij} - \mu_j}{\sigma_j}\]
